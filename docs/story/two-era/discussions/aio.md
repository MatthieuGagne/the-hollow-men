# Discussion — Āio

**Question:** Their file says *Investigation Entry: TBD*. Relocating them to the revolution
(decision 13) means that hook has to be designed in the era instead — where are they when the
party arrives, and why do they leave with it?
**Status:** resolved in discussion · 2026-11-20

**Held from their file:** the tradition predates first contact itself; no sigils, no cyberware,
not as a political stance; output loops inward, so the assessment reads zero and they are filed as
a non-practitioner; the wound is **administrative, never vengeful** — *the machine didn't hate it,
it processed it.* Decision 14: the wound spans **both regimes**, the Patrimony's tithe and the
founding census.

---

## Where the party finds them — mid-collapse `[DECIDED]`

The community is **coming apart as the party arrives**. The tithe is gone, the census has arrived,
people are scattering — and Āio is the one staying.

**The school is founded in front of the player.** Out of necessity, not ceremony: the regime
changed, the extraction didn't, and the forms will die with the generation that still knows them
unless somebody holds a room open.

### Why this and not the alternatives

- It is the only version where what the player watches is a **decision**, not a catastrophe. Āio
  is not a victim the party collects.
- It keeps them un-reactive, which their file insists on. **The census does not make them.** They
  were already this; the census only made the school necessary.
- *Grief made functional* — already the phrase in `world.md` — is shown being manufactured rather
  than described.
- It is what makes the present-day payoff land. The Fringe dojo two centuries later is the same
  room, still running, taught slightly wrong. That only hurts if we watched it begin.

**Rejected:** arriving before the collapse (party watches the wound happen — brutal, but makes
them reactive); arriving after the school exists (cleanest recruitment, skips the best material);
the school as a safehouse the party needs (functional, mechanical).

---

## Why they leave it — they hand it over, and fail to return `[DECIDED]`

**A, with a thread of B.** A senior student takes the school, with instructions and a timeframe.
Āio crosses for a bounded reason — the machine that processed their people is what the party is
chasing, and it is the first time the wound has ever had something to aim at.

**And the door does not give them the trip back.** Doors are rare; the iron rule is absolute. They
do not abandon the school. **They fail to return to it.**

### What this buys

| | |
|---|---|
| **The dojo payoff is transformed** | It stops being a pleasant epilogue. The Fringe dojo is the *only way Āio will ever learn what happened to the school* — and the answer is *it survived*, run by strangers, teaching their forms slightly wrong. Best possible outcome and a quiet grief nobody else in the party can see. |
| **The forms measure the absence** | How wrong the forms have drifted is the measure of how long they have been gone. |
| **The iron rule becomes personal** | Everyone else cannot change the past. **Āio cannot go home.** They are the only character whose relationship to the rule is lived rather than philosophical. |
| **The targeting stays administrative** | They follow the *machine*, not a culprit. No avenger arc; the thing they are chasing has no face, which is the point of the wound. |

**Rejected:** following one specific person (makes the arc a rescue the game then has to resolve);
crossing to secure the school's future (paradoxical — the past cannot be changed, so the motive is
built on a rule the game says does not work).

## Pairing: Āio and Paz

Both era-native, both watched extraction survive a regime change — and they are the same wound
argued from opposite ends.

| Paz | Āio |
|---|---|
| Wrote the broadsheet | Was never moved by it |
| Believed the rising, and lost | Had already watched one regime replace another and take the same thing |
| Left a list she was on | Left a room they built |
| Can go back and never does | Cannot go back and would |

They are the only two people in the present who remember the same century, and they remember it
completely differently. Neither is wrong.
