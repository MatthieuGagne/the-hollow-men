# Discussion — The Two Incidents

**Question:** Why did the revolution start, why did it end in a licensing charter, and why is
General Varenne the way he is?
**Status:** in discussion · opened 2026-11-17
**Forced by:** the existing design starts the revolution on *ideology* and ends it on *procedure*.
Neither is epic, and neither makes the outcome feel inevitable in retrospect. It also inherits a
problem from `first-contact.md`: once the tithe is allowed to have *worked*, nothing in the era
makes the Patrimony look monstrous, and the radicals read as reckless.

---

## The shape: two incidents, opposite polarity

| | Incident | Causes |
|---|---|---|
| **One** | The Patrimony tithes children. A district's generation goes hollow. | **The revolution.** |
| **Two** | A survivor of that tithe comes apart after abolition. | **The charter.** |

Both sides earn their position with blood. Nobody is wrong at the moment they act. That is the
difference between a committee vote and a tragedy.

Rejected structures: one ambiguous incident read two ways (hard to stage, player feels cheated);
a post-abolition incident only (leaves the revolution's own origin thin); a chain of three or more
(diffuses into texture, no single image the player carries).

---

## Incident One — The Child Tithe

**What happens.** A bad year. The quota rises. The order begins tithing **children**, because
untrained output is the cheapest thing to take: it does not resist, it does not notice, and a
child has no professional life to disrupt. Not malice — **actuarial**.

They pilot it in **one district**, the way you pilot any policy before extending it. One
generation of that quarter grows up **blank** — breathing, functional, answering questions
correctly, and gone in exactly the way the present-day calibrated describe the quiet.

**The cover-up is the actual crime.** The Patrimony does not admit an error. It declares the
district **blessed**: the children who gave most are nearest the Patron. Sanctification is how the
quarter gets quarantined, and quarantine is how it gets forgotten.

Admission was never available to them — conceding that the tithe has a *limit* means conceding it
is not holy. (Consistent with the sincere-insurers reading in `first-contact.md`: they are not
cynics, which is why they cannot be honest.)

**The proof (folded in from the rejected "quota letters" option).** The party finds the order's
internal correspondence in the era: tolerance figures known, quota raised anyway, for years. The
word *children* appears nowhere. The clerks wrote **"untrained yield"** — the ancestors of the
clerks who draft consent forms, with the house style already in place.

### What this buys

- **The title becomes a historical event.** *The Hollow Men* stops being a metaphor about the
  present. **Constraint:** the era never speaks the phrase and neither does the Bureau. The party
  coins it, standing in that quarter — the player hears the game's title said aloud two centuries
  before the game they are playing.
- **The ancestor of Output Calibration.** The quieting is this refined into procedure; calibration
  is the quieting with a diagnostic code. The Bureau's worst instrument begins as a cost-saving
  measure applied to children.
- **A location, not a grievance.** The quarantined quarter is walkable — the one place in the era
  where the entity's work is visible without descending to the Ruins.
- **Āio's wound gets its specific.** Their file already says the tithe *and* the founding census
  took from their people. Now there is an event, not only a pattern.

## Incident Two — The Survivor

**Who.** Not one of the blanked. **The one it failed on.** Partially drained as a child, never
trained, carrying output nobody metered and nobody taught.

**Why it happens when it happens.** After abolition there is no Patrimony left to contain them and
no Bureau yet to license them. The revolution's victory removes the only thing that was holding
them. They come apart, and take a great deal of the city with them.

### Why this is the version worth having

The Patrimony's crime **creates the weapon that justifies the Bureau.** The two atrocities are one
causal chain, not two events:

> The order tithes children to save money. The city rises in rage over it. Abolition frees
> everyone — including the one person who should never have been left unheld. The catastrophe
> that follows writes a charter that re-institutes the harvest under a better name.

Every faction gets what it deserved, and the city gets the Bureau. **The moderates are right
because the radicals were right.**

Rejected perpetrators: a radical true believer proving a point (makes the radicals foolish rather
than tragic); an ordinary practitioner with nothing collecting (honest about the mechanic, but
cold); Patrimony loyalists acting deliberately (lets the revolution off the hook entirely).

## The Night of the Lamps — Incident Two, staged

*Working title, era-side. Six beats. Settled: he **fails**, and then he **kills** — B+C.*

**1. The quarter wakes up.** The survivor does not attack. They **come apart**, and the
coming-apart has a radius. It begins in the sanctified quarter, where they grew up, where the rest
of their generation is still standing around breathing and blank. The city's first sign is that
the blank ones have begun **moving in step**. Whatever is unspooling out of this one person
reaches the others, and the others have nothing left in them to refuse with.

**2. The General does the correct thing.** Cordon, evacuate outward, contain inward. Textbook —
and designed for an enemy that occupies space, where this one occupies *people*. The cordon holds
about an hour, then it is inside the cordon, because it was always inside the cordon: it came in
on the men he posted there.

**3. The hour he loses.** The beat that makes him. He is not stupid and not slow; he is **out of
category**. Every instrument he has — discipline, position, numbers, nerve — is the wrong
instrument, and he spends an hour discovering that at the cost of the people who trusted him most.
His own troops, from the war that just ended, standing in a street they were told was safe.

There is no tactical error to point at. That is the cruelty. A man who has never lost has no
framework for *this was not losable.*

**4. The lamps.** Permanent night; the quarter runs on oil. Somebody — **not him** — fires the
district to break the spread. It half-works. The catastrophe acquires its image: burning rain, a
quarter alight under a sky that never lightens, and figures walking in step through it,
unbothered, because nobody is home to mind the fire. That is what the party carries out of the
era, and what the city carries for two centuries.

**5. He finds them, and it is already over.** The survivor is spent. A young adult sitting in the
wet, **lucid** — probably for the first time in the whole event — and aware of what has happened.
The only person in the city who both caused this and understands it. And no longer dangerous.
Nothing is about to happen.

**They get a few lines. Not a monologue.** A tragic speech would make them a character the player
grieves as a person, which weakens the horror: the point is that the city never learned who they
were. Their **name** appears in the Accountants' minutes, not in their own mouth.

**6. He kills them anyway.** With the blade — unregistered, no serial, the one that ends up in an
archive and then on Kurtz's hip two centuries later.

He gives a different reason at different points for the rest of his life:

| Reason | Register |
|---|---|
| A thing that did this cannot be allowed to exist | policy |
| They asked him to | mercy |
| He had watched his own men die for nothing and needed the night to have an ending | grief |
| A living survivor means testimony, testimony means the city learns the Patrimony made this, and the city is two days from tearing itself apart | **statecraft** |

The last is the one he never says aloud, and the one the party can prove from the Accountants'
minutes.

## Knock-on: calibration's father moves

B+C means the General invents nothing, so the charter's instrument needs another origin. It is
better placed anyway:

**The quieting is the civilian answer to a soldier's failure.** The Committee's argument writes
itself — *the army could not stop it; the army could only kill it afterward; we will not be
reduced to that again.* The quieting is drafted as an explicitly **humane alternative to what the
General did.** Its two-century career begins as the thing invented so that nobody would ever again
have to put a blade in a victim.

The clerk who drafts it is sincerely preventing a murder. That is why calibration keeps its
consent form, its clinical pathway, its forty minutes and its follow-up schedule: the Bureau's
worst instrument was designed by people reacting **in horror at violence**.

## Consequences for General Varenne

His file says the Accountants court him because *"the Committee cannot control what the army
loves,"* and that his break is the Descent — he looks, it looks back.

**He is no longer persuaded in a room.** He arrives at the Descent carrying two facts: *I could
not stop it*, and *I executed a victim in the rain and called it four different things.* Then he
looks, and what looks back is the thing that made the tithe that made the child that made the
night. The offer — be the one who holds this together — lands on a man who already believes nobody
is holding anything. The Descent does not convert him; it **confirms** him.

**It makes his speech honest.** *"The people must be licensed for their own protection"* is, in
his mouth, a sentence about one specific person he killed. He is not wrong. He is the only man in
the chamber who has seen what the alternative costs — and he is lying about his reasons while
telling the truth about the stakes.

**And the blade pays off.** Kurtz carries it unknowing: the weapon that killed a tithed child,
worn as an accessory by a man who thinks the coat is a promotion.
