## Why this shape

- **Era and present alternate rather than interleave.** Each episode has a home century, so the
  player always knows which game they are in — and the present never goes dark, because Kos is
  working it.
- **The breaks land where FF6 puts them.** Episode 3 splits the party; Episode 5 is a
  single-character episode that re-forms it. That is the midpoint collapse the old act structure
  wanted, relocated into the episodic frame.
- **One reveal per episode:** the quarter (2), Paz's origin (3), the quieting's invention (4), the
  tether rule (5), the charter and the coat (6), what the ending actually means (7).
- **Door escalation has a slot.** An *attended* door fits in 6, or between 6 and 7 — the party
  makes its last crossing knowing the wound noticed.
- **Late episodes lose people rather than gain them.** Āio and Paz arrive early, which
  front-loads the era cast deliberately.

## Decisions this forces

**Vesper's roster slot.** `roster.md` lists her as a permanent playable member recruited late, but
A2 ruled out a playable era stretch. Provisional resolution: **she is recovered present-side in
Episode 7** — recovered, not rescued, and late enough that she is an endgame presence rather than
a companion. The alternative is cutting her from the playable ten and letting the roster sit at
nine. **Not settled.**

**Episode 2's party is thin.** First crossing is Reid, Iris, Karim and Margot, with no era-native
guide until Āio joins mid-episode. Worth checking it does not feel underpowered.

**Alternative cut if seven runs long:** merge 5 into 4, making Gideon's break the present-side half
of the Lamps episode. Six episodes, cheaper — at the cost of Gideon's own episode, and he is the
one character whose turn earns one.
