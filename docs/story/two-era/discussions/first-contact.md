# Discussion — First Contact and the Prehistory

**Question:** What happened before the revolution? How did the entity end up in the state the
game finds it in, and how did an accident become the Patrimony's holy tithe?
**Status:** in discussion · opened 2026-11-17
**Scope:** history only. No mechanics. Nothing here is canon.

---

## The state of the entity: stranded, then inert

It attempted the crossing **long before people**, and failed. It is stuck partway between the
physical and information registers — not waiting out of patience, but out of **inability**. The
Ruins are the crash site.

Stranded, it went inert. Not asleep by choice: the way a body in shock goes still.

### Why this is the right answer

- A thing that could cross freely would not need a city, a charter, a Bureau, or a Grade system.
  Strandedness is what makes the entire two-century machine *necessary* rather than decorative.
- It upgrades an existing canon line. `concept.md`: *"It did not simply wait — it built, slowly,
  through human proxies, across generations."* That is the behaviour of something that woke up
  unable to move and had to use other people's hands.
- It retro-explains the door model settled in session 3: a thing already torn is a thing that
  scars when you cut it again.
- **It reframes the ending.** Stranding it between registers stops being a clever compromise and
  becomes *restoring the status quo ante* — putting it back in the condition it was in before we
  touched it. The best available ending is **undoing first contact**.

### The one adjustment this forces

`world.md` says the entity *"drew settlement — it wanted to be built around."* Under a stranded,
inert entity, the drawing must become **passive, not intentional**. What leaks from something
stuck halfway through is not a lure, it is a **wound bleeding into the local register**, and human
minds are exactly the instrument that registers that bleed.

Nobody heard a call. People felt a place that felt *significant* — and significance is enough.
Settlements form around the uncanny. It drew settlement the way a magnet draws filings: no wanting
involved.

> **Propagation note:** "it wanted to be built around" in `world.md` would need rewording if this
> is approved. The fact survives; the intention does not.

## The waking: proximity, not trespass

Minds in proximity, in numbers, over generations, were the one input a stranded thing could still
use. That is the waking. Not malevolence — **stimulus**.

No one did anything wrong. Nobody trespassed, nobody touched it, nobody made a bargain. **The
crime is demographic:** we were there, in numbers, paying attention. Humanity is culpable in the
Lovecraftian sense — none of this happens if we don't gather and look.

---

## The first expedition: a death, observed

An expedition goes down into what will later be called the Ruins. Someone **dies** — a fall, a
collapse, the ordinary way people die underground. **An accident, never an offering.**

And the thing takes what spills.

That is the load-bearing moment of the whole prehistory. The survivors watch a death become an
**input**. They made no bargain, stole no relic, received no reply. They observed a mechanism, and
came back up understanding one fact nobody else in the world had:

> **It takes this, and this is how it arrives.**

Two centuries of ley architecture are the industrialisation of an accident witnessed once.

### What WARDEN actually inherits

**A method.** Not a relic, not a secret, not a contract. That is harder to destroy than any of
them: you can burn a document and bury an object, but you cannot un-know what something eats.

### Deliberate exclusions

| Excluded | Why |
|---|---|
| **A first-contact artifact** | Kurtz's blade "came out of the archive" and predates the Bureau — the Patrimony era already covers that. One artifact carrying two centuries of provenance is elegant; carrying all of history is clutter. |
| **The entity speaking** | It never speaks, start to finish. No reply, no bargain, no contract. Every "relationship" the city believes it has is a human invention laid over a mechanism. |
| **A named first victim** | No name, no ceremony, no martyr. The first death is anonymous, and that is the noir of it. |

## From accident to religion: appeasement that acquired evidence

How "it takes this" becomes the Patrimony's holy tithe, in two movements:

**1. Appeasement (the grim part).** More deaths near the site. They get read as the thing being
*owed*. Giving becomes prevention. Nobody can disprove it, because the city keeps standing. The
Patrimony's founders are not cynics — they are **insurers**.

**2. Evidence (the dangerous part).** And then something actually got better. The site quieted.
The perpetual night became liveable. The settlement grew where it had no business growing. The
appeasement **acquired evidence**, and gratitude layered on top of fear.

### Why this combination and not the alternatives

The Patrimony ends up **entirely sincere, entirely correct about the mechanism, and wrong about
every reason.** That is the exact shape of the present-day Bureau — an institution that believes
its own framing, is right that the dangers are real, and is serving something it does not
understand.

Same error, three centuries apart. That symmetry is the two-era structure's whole argument.

It also makes the revolution's abolition of the tithe **genuinely reckless**, not obviously
correct. The radicals are right about the theft and wrong about the risk. The moderates are wrong
about the solution and not wrong about the danger. Nobody in the revolution is simply correct —
which is the only way that era is worth four visits.

Rejected: **(C) WARDEN's ancestors engineered the religion deliberately** — makes two centuries of
faith a hoax, and the project's rule is that institutions believe their own framing.
**(D) nobody decided, it just accreted** — most realistic, least playable.

## The full chronology

| Period | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Before people** | The entity attempts the crossing. It fails. Stranded between registers, it goes inert. |
| **The bleed** | Its wound leaks into the local register. Human minds read the leak as significance. Settlement accretes around the uncanny. |
| **The waking** | Minds in proximity, in numbers, over generations. Stimulus, not summons. |
| **The first expedition** | A descent into the Ruins. An accidental death. The survivors watch it become an input. |
| **The method** | The survivors' inheritors keep what they learned. They become WARDEN. |
| **Appeasement** | Deaths read as debt. Giving becomes prevention. Insurance becomes rite. |
| **Evidence** | The night becomes liveable, the site quiets, the city grows. The rite acquires proof. |
| **The Patrimony** | A temple-mercantile order tithing psychic output to the Deep Patron, openly, as religion. Reliquaries as collection. |
| **The revolution** | *(see `approaches.md`)* The tithe is abolished. The truth is printed. |
| **The founding** | The charter. The harvest at industrial scale — which cuts the doors. |

## Settled in this session

**Chronology approved as drafted**, including the "it actually worked" seam — the tithe produced
a real benefit, so abolishing it is genuinely reckless.

**The prehistory is spoken aloud.** WARDEN knows it fully, and at some point someone says it.
This is a deliberate break from the game's usual withholding, and it carries two constraints:

- **The entity still never speaks.** The account is a *human* account of a mechanism. Whoever
  delivers it is describing an accident their institution has been exploiting for centuries —
  not relaying a message.
- **It must be told as administration, not revelation.** The horror of the Bureau is procedural;
  the prehistory should arrive the same way — flat, bored, the way you explain a load-bearing
  wall. The worst possible version is a villain monologue in a cathedral.

**Āio's tradition predates first contact itself.** Not merely older than the Patrimony — older
than the entity's entire involvement in human affairs. It did not form in the bleed, which is
exactly why the entity has no model for it (already canon in `aio.md`). Their islands are
somewhere the wound never reached, and `aio.md` already notes the ley terminal network "doesn't
reach water well."
