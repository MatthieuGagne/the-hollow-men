# Discussion — Gideon

**Question:** The tether rule makes his moral break and his physical eligibility to cross the
*same event*. His file leaves the break itself TBD. What triggers it, and does he know what it
unlocks?
**Status:** resolved in discussion · 2026-11-20

**Held from his file:** not corrupt, not cruel — loyal to something that was always a machine, and
the loyalty is genuine. The framework (*the institution requires difficult tools, the alternative
is worse, someone has to hold the line*) is load-bearing, and examining it means dismantling his
identity. **The moment he can no longer write a report that makes sense to himself is the moment
he stops being Bureau.** Margot is his Academy classmate; he knew what her referral triggered and
said nothing. After the Great Below he files *investigation ongoing* — and his file notes Kurtz
will eventually notice that flag.

---

## He does not know what leaving unlocks `[DECIDED]`

Nobody tells him that leaving the Bureau is what would let him cross. **He leaves because he can
no longer write the report.** Then a door works for him, and that is how he learns what being
licensed actually cost.

### Why this and not the alternatives

- **The break has to be uncontaminated.** If he knows crossing is the prize, his defection becomes
  a transaction, and the most sincere character in the cast is bought.
- **The reveal lands as horror rather than reward.** The Bureau owned his movement, and he never
  knew he was holding still. Every licensed practitioner in NOX is pinned to one century; the
  registry is a leash; he spent a career administering it.
- **Second-order beat, available for free:** he has watched calibrations. Under the tether rule,
  everyone he helped process was not only drained but **nailed down** — and he can only understand
  that after he is free.

**Rejected:** the party tells him (makes them recruiters and the choice a bribe; Reid would not
use it); he deduces it himself from who crosses and who doesn't (very him, but then he chooses
with the answer in hand); the Bureau tells him as a loyalty test (opens B5 in the worst place and
shows a card the Bureau should keep).

---

## The report he can't write `[DECIDED]`

**Sequencing constraint:** he must break **in the present**. He cannot reach the era until after
the break, so the trigger cannot be anything he witnesses there.

### Cause — the disappearance cohort

Kurtz's operation arrives on his desk as **routine paperwork**: fully calibrated Grade III+
practitioners, transferred, no subsequent output readings, no assessments filed. He is asked to
write the clean version of people being consumed.

> He can write the report truthfully, or he can write it correctly. For the first time in his
> life, those are different documents.

His faith rests on the belief that **procedure describes reality**. Here, procedure exists
*specifically in order not to*. The horror is a missing field on a form — exactly the register the
Bureau's horror is supposed to operate in.

It also indicts the **institution** rather than a person, which is the only kind of break that
dismantles a framework instead of merely straining it.

### Pressure — Margot, in the room, not the reason

The order to bring in his Academy classmate lands the same week, while he is holding the cohort
file. The institution asks for both in one breath.

- The cohort tells him **the Bureau is false.**
- Margot tells him **what acting on that costs.**

She is present and she is not the cause. If she were, his arc would be about loyalty to a friend
rather than about an institution being untrue — a smaller story, and one his file does not
support.

**Rejected:** a calibration he must personally authorise on an unregistered kid (maximum cruelty,
but it duplicates Margot's wound almost exactly — two characters broken by the same form); Kos
handed to him for processing (ties the two tether-opposites together, but requires Kos captured,
which costs the present-side plot).

---

## The sequence, end to end

1. He files *investigation ongoing* after the Great Below. Kurtz notices the flag.
2. The cohort file arrives as routine work — possibly *because* he was noticed.
3. The order on Margot lands in the same window.
4. He cannot write the report. He stops being Bureau.
5. **Then** a door works for him — and the cohort is reframed: those people were not only emptied,
   they were **pinned**, and he helped.
