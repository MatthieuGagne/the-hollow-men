# Discussion — Travel Rules

**Question (A1):** Who can cross between centuries, and why doesn't everyone?
**Status:** in discussion · opened 2026-11-15
**Forced by:** decisions 15 and 17 — Paz crossed forward alone, Vesper crossed back alone.
Crossing is something *individuals* do, so the game needs a rule or the question has no answer.

---
## Session 3 — 2026-11-15 — what a door is (draft, agreed in conversation)

The tether rule says who *can* cross. It never said what they cross *through*. Settled here:

**Doors are wounds in the entity, not places in the city.** The between-space is its body;
a door is a scar. Crossing is a trespass inside the thing.

**The scars were cut by the founding harvest, during the revolution.** Not by the revolution
itself and not by the entity's own surfacing — by the first industrial-scale extraction, the
one the city's charter authorised. Consequences that fall out for free:

- **Why only one era.** A wound connects only to the moment it was made. There is no medieval
  NOX to visit because nobody was cutting then. The era is the scar tissue's address — and that
  address is exactly where the charter, the Gold Coat and WARDEN's origin all sit.
- **Why doors are rare, and why they move.** They exist only where the harvest cut deep. A scar
  marks where it was hurt, not where it lives.
- **The tether rule gets a mechanism.** Metered output runs to the ley architecture; the
  architecture feeds the entity. Being metered means being held by the same thing whose body you
  would have to walk through. The leash now has a visible other end.
- **Vesper, in character.** She didn't find a passage. She found a wound and went in.
- **The thesis.** The founding crime cut the holes that are the only way to go back and witness
  the founding crime. *The crime is the evidence of itself.*

**New canon fact requiring approval before propagation:** the entity can be wounded, and
two centuries of harvest have been keeping those wounds open.

### Sub-question — settled: what the entity does about crossers

**A-then-B.** The *stated* rule is A: scar tissue is numb, it cannot feel you. Said plainly
in-world by Soraya or Sabine on an early crossing — which discharges objection 4. What turns
out to be true is B: the wound is not as dead as they said, and at some point it is **attended**.

**Attention is story-scripted, never usage-tracked.** No per-door use count, no threshold, no
accumulating meter. The beat lands when the narrative places it — a door that worked a hundred
times and a door used once are equally liable, because nothing is being counted. When the beat
comes, **the door cannot be used**, and that is a plot event, not a cost the player paid.

Why the counter was rejected: it implies attention accrues in proportion to traffic, which is a
*mechanism* — players would ration crossings to stay under it, and the entity would become a
system to be managed rather than a thing that notices when it notices.

Rejected: (C) indifference, no escalation curve; (D) the entity permits because it wants
witnesses — the "antagonist as taxi" failure mode.

The structural point: **the rule the game teaches is the rule that stops being true.** That is
the horror shape the project already runs on everywhere else.

---

## The proposal on the table

**Metered output is a tether.**

A practitioner whose output runs through the ley architecture is anchored to the moment the
architecture is collecting in. The harvest doesn't only take your power — **it takes your
ability to leave.** Only the unmetered cross.

### Why it is attractive

1. **It converts the game's thesis into a mechanic.** The Bureau's extraction is revealed to
   do something worse than theft: it pins people to a single present. Everyone who can move
   through time is someone the system failed to capture.
2. **The roster already sorts cleanly by it** — nobody designed it that way. Every character's
   existing Bureau status in `_overview.md` predicts their travel status without edits.
3. **Reid crosses because the system deemed him beneath it.** The man with no Grade, no
   output, no Academy is free precisely because nothing ever wanted anything from him. That
   is the noir thesis in one sentence, and it is his disgrace reframed as the thing that saves him.
4. **Gideon's first crossing *is* his arc break.** His file says he stops being Bureau the
   moment he can no longer write a report that makes sense to himself. Under this rule, the
   moment he can cross is the moment he is no longer theirs. Symbolic becomes mechanical.

---

## The hole, and a fix

**The hole:** the first draft blocked Kos because *the entity* tethers him, and allowed
Vesper because her anchor was revoked. But the entity is in both of them. That is
inconsistent — the same cause produced opposite results.

**The fix (strong, and it improves the story):** **Kos is blocked by the Bureau, not the entity.**

His calibration sigil is still in him. `bureau.md` says a calibration sigil is *deep-bonded —
anchored below the interface layer the practitioner can reach.* He is, technically, **the
most thoroughly metered person in the cast.** The procedure failed in its purpose but
succeeded in its plumbing. He cannot be unmetered because he cannot reach it.

And Vesper is the exact inverse: **the Bureau revoked her anchor, and that is what let her go.**
They cut her loose as a punishment and it freed her from the century.

That symmetry is worth having on its own:

> The Bureau trapped the man it failed to calibrate, and freed the woman it tried to erase.

It also reframes Kos's wound. It is no longer "the entity holds me" — it is "the institution
is still inside me, and the one door out of this century is the one thing it still locks."

## Objections not yet resolved

1. **If no output means no tether, the city is full of non-practitioners.** Why isn't
   crossing common? → Proposed second condition: **you need an absent tether *and* a door.**
   Doors are vanishingly rare — Meridian thin spots, Lain's resonance, the passage Vesper
   left ajar. The tether rule explains who *can* use a door; it was never meant to explain
   why doors are rare. Needs confirming as the intended reading.
2. **Does WARDEN know?** If metering tethers, WARDEN almost certainly knows, which makes
   control-of-movement part of the harvest's purpose. That is a thematic upgrade but it
   shifts the Bureau's reason to exist. Options: (a) side effect WARDEN later exploited;
   (b) intended from the founding — the charter pins people deliberately. (b) is darker and
   gives the era's charter a second, hidden function.
3. **Margot was metered for twelve years before jailbreaking.** Does cutting the channel
   free her completely, or does prior metering leave residue? A cost here is available and
   possibly valuable — she crosses, but she is the only one who can *feel* the tether's
   absence, like a removed limb.
4. **Is the rule legible to the player?** A rule nobody can infer is a rule that feels
   arbitrary. Needs an in-world moment where it is stated plainly — probably Sabine or
   Soraya, both of whom read the infrastructure.
5. **Does it make the party's composition feel pre-determined?** If travel eligibility is
   fixed by backstory, episode party selection is partly on rails. May be fine; worth
   checking against the episode map (B3).

---

## Alternatives not yet explored

- **No rule — doors are just rare.** Anyone can cross if they find a way. Simplest; loses
  the thesis payoff and makes Kos's exclusion arbitrary.
- **The entity chooses.** Crossing is permitted by the thing in the between-space. Creepier,
  but it makes the party's mobility a gift from the antagonist, which undercuts agency.
- **Cost-based rather than eligibility-based.** Anyone can cross; it takes something from
  you each time. More flexible for episode design, weaker thematically.
