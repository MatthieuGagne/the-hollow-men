# Party Overview

Ten-character ensemble. Each character has a different relationship to the Bureau's system and to the revelation that their power was always their own.

Each also owns a **unique combat verb** — no character is a union of two others' lists. Full rules and ability tables in `docs/design/mechanics.md` § Character Abilities. Two further slots (11 and 12) are intended and undesigned.

| Class | Character | Verb | Bureau Status |
|-------|-----------|------|---------------|

| Summoner | Vesper | **Summon** — alternating turns with the being | Unlicensed — joins later |

## Class Notes

**Black Mage (Margot)** — Grade IV. Academy-trained; the Bureau's claimed asset. Jailbroke after filing a welfare referral that triggered the calibration of someone she loved. The swap was not preemptive self-preservation — it was the only response she had to something she couldn't undo. The ideology came after, built around the guilt. Mechanically she is the artillery: biggest numbers in the game on the slowest gauge, and the only character who can **Overdraw** — exceed her metered output on purpose and visibly pay for it, exactly as the Wave 3 Captain does in the tutorial fight. Also the only character who exploits elemental/register weaknesses. Her Bureau limit break is proper, sanctioned technique. Her jailbroken limit break is who she actually is underneath it.

**Bard (Paz)** — psychic output expresses through performance — specifically a heavily modified electric guitar. The Bureau never thought to meter that channel, which means no Grade on file — the ley terminals register something but the assessment protocol doesn't know what to do with it. Anti-establishment against the whole system (Bureau, megacorps, WARDEN) by structural experience as much as intent. **Sets** are timed, layerable, and battlefield-wide: every one buffs all allies and debuffs all enemies in the same action. Uptempo makes her the party's only ally-gauge accelerator.

**Martial Artist (Āio)** — no sigils, no cyberware — the tradition predates the Bureau's entire framework, the refusal isn't reactive. Output loops inward as physical enhancement. Bureau assessment protocol requires outward resonance; reading returns zero, filed as non-practitioner, forgotten. Tradition is completely orthogonal to the entity — never contaminated by its machine, so she takes reduced Psychic Drain and partially ignores entity resistances. She fights in **Forms**: one stance at a time, each redefining her basic attack and her gauge speed. Switching costs a turn; attacking inside a Form is free. The one party member operating from a framework the entity has no model for.

**Occultist (Kos / Koschei)** — entity-touched via a failed Output Calibration. The entity used the bonding process as a channel — something came through the other way. **Echo**: he learns abilities only by having them used on him, entity and entity-adjacent attacks only. Learning is automatic — survive the hit, keep the attack. He joins late, already carrying a meaningful list, because a party member who is useless for ten hours gets benched and a benched character learns nothing. His list isn't a collection, it's a record of contact. Most terrifying fighter in the party by Act 3. Bureau status: classified, escaped. Hiding in the Meridian.

**Defender (Gideon)** — Grade III. Formerly conceived as a fighter/healer hybrid; that made him a Red Mage, a union of Reid and Karim with nothing of his own, so the healing is gone. He is the wall: highest HP and DEF in the cast, **Interpose** to absorb and redirect damage aimed at allies, Bulwark, Hold the Line, and real secondary damage gated behind a slow gauge. Interpose consumes his turn — he is never simultaneously the wall and the hammer. Field enforcer, occasionally Assessment Division. Academy-trained; the institution worked for him at the level he was given access to. Not corrupt — loyal to something that was always a machine. Has witnessed calibrations and rationalized them. Margot's Academy classmate; knew what her report triggered and said nothing. The most heartbreaking arc because he is not wrong to believe in justice. He just put it in the wrong institution.

**Summoner (Vesper)** — joins the party later. Unique mechanic: shares turns with her being. Every turn can be split between Vesper and the being. The being acts on borrowed output. The being is still there underneath the entity's presence — still trying to reach her, still the thread Reid can pull.

**Hacker (Soraya)** — hacks enemy cyberware and sigils to malfunction and backfire. Enemies attack their own allies, misfired sigils damage their casters, hacks cascade into adjacent systems. Almost no direct damage — the most indirect fighter in the party. Her effects fire when the hacked target acts, not on her own turn: she plays the enemy's clock. Against entities she retargets the information layer she has mapped in the deep Grid over decades — she never goes dark. Knows the entity's shape without knowing its name. Bureau has no file on her; she erases her own Grid activity logs.

**Fighter (Reid)** — the only character with no PP bar. Abilities cost HP or nothing; Psychic Drain hits his HP instead of PP. His gauge fills faster the lower his HP is, so playing him well means deliberately running low. **Pressure** stacks on a target and erodes its DEF and RES *for the whole party* — who Reid is working on organizes every fight. Late-game he finally takes a sigil and gains a PP bar, which after thirty hours reads as a loss rather than an upgrade.

**Thief (Iris)** — fastest unit in the cast, two to three actions per ally turn. **Steal** is a ladder, not a side action: Lift (items) → Skim (PP) → Strip (a buff, transferred to an ally) → Palm (the enemy's queued action — they lose the turn, she gets it). A failed steal still reveals the target's next action in the turn order, so it is never a wasted turn. Never registered: she has no licensed limit break at all, only a jailbroken one, from day one.

**Medic (Karim)** — no healing magic and no innate spell list. He carries **devices**, three slots, chosen between fights and found, bought or upgraded during investigation. Stim, Suture, Stabilizer Rig, Defibrillator, Dampener. He is the only in-combat source of PP restoration, which makes him structurally essential in entity fights where Psychic Drain is the damage type. The one party member whose build is a function of where you've been.
