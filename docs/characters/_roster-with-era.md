# Full Roster — Two-Era Structure

> **Status:** Discussion draft, 2026-11-15. Nothing here is canon. Existing character files
> are unmodified. New characters are marked `[NEW]` and all working names are flagged.
> This document exists so the roster can be judged as a whole before anything is decided.

**Structure assumed:** episodic, not three-act. Episode 1 ≈ the existing Act 1 (opening
sequence + Cases 1–4), re-cut to end on the time-travel hook. Chrono Trigger topology —
characters recruited in the revolution era **come forward** and stay. Permanent playable
roster holds at ten; the named cast grows past ten through temporary and NPC tiers.

---

**No cuts required.** Relocating Āio and Paz to the revolution does the rebalancing on its
own. Both relocations *strengthen* beats already written — details below.

### Āio — relocated to the past

Their file already says the tradition "predates the Bureau's entire framework," that they
are "the one party member operating from a framework the entity has no model for," and
that the tradition "was never contaminated by the system the entity built." Their dojo in
the Fringe is described in `world.md` as a location that predates the Bureau, with first
visit marked *"TBD — Case 5 or later."* They have no present-day recruitment hook in the
docs because the hook is marked TBD.

**The change:** Āio is a revolution-era fighter. The Bureau's administrative destruction of
their community — the core wound, currently a Bureau sweep — becomes the **Patrimony's**
tithe-collection of their island community, or the new Office of Civic Output's first
census. Same wound, same machine, two hundred years earlier: *the machine didn't hate it,
it processed it.* That sentence works in either century without edit.

**The payoff:** they found the school in the era. They come forward with the party. The
dojo is **still standing in the Fringe**, run by inheritors who never knew them, teaching
their forms slightly wrong. The grief-made-functional place outlived the grief and the
person. Nothing in `world.md` needs to change — it already describes the dojo as older
than the Bureau.

### Paz — relocated to the past

Iris's file: she is "a fan of Paz specifically, whose work she knows from the Sprawl
underground before they ever meet," and "Paz doesn't know Iris exists. The asymmetry is the
dynamic."

**If Paz is a revolutionary,** Iris is a fan of **two-hundred-year-old banned songs that
survived in the underground**. The asymmetry stops being a fandom joke and becomes the best
scene available to either character: Paz learns her music outlived the revolution, the
Bureau, and herself — and that the woman in front of her knows every word.

Her core wound (the Regularization — "they processed it, forms filed correctly, several
didn't come back") transfers intact to the era's first census. Her anti-establishment
position stops being a stance and becomes biography: she fought the founding and lost.
Her "won't examine" — that she moves people without consent the way the Bureau harvests
without consent — is *sharper* in a revolution, where that is literally what a
revolutionary song does.

**Cost:** her Meridian childhood and the Kos recognition beat ("she recognizes him before
he recognizes her") are lost or rewritten. That's the real price of this relocation.

### Soraya — reworked for the era

The revolution has no Grid. It has the **Patrimony's reliquary network** `[NEW]` — an
analog harvest grid of linked tithe-vessels, the ley architecture's first physical draft.
With no Grid to muffle it, the entity's information register sits *nearer the surface*.

In the present she reached the entity through decades of careful mapping. In the past it
is simply **bare**. Her kit works; the vector is rawer and more dangerous. This also
explains the hook that finds the era: her forty-seven terminals include signatures that
**predate their own installation dates** — she has been reading a network older than the
Bureau without knowing it.

### Kos — open question

He is entity-touched. In the era the entity is nearer the surface and the Meridian — the
only place what's in him goes quiet — does not yet exist in its modern form. Two options,
both good, and they are mutually exclusive:

- **He can't travel.** The past is unbearable; what came through during his calibration
  gets *louder*. He stays present-side. Makes the era feel genuinely dangerous.
- **He's the only one who can find the door.** The thing in him remembers the century.
  He's the party's compass and it costs him every crossing.

### Vesper — open question

The entity is in her and the entity is **across time**. Whether she can be glimpsed in the
revolution — the same face, in a crowd, two hundred years early — is the single biggest
unexploited idea in this structure. It would make the manifestation something that has
always been happening rather than something arriving. **Flagged, not decided.** Her file's
rule stands: every scene is a question of which presence is speaking.

## Tier 2 — Temporary Playable (era guests)

Playable across era visits, lost by design. This is the FF6 General Leo slot: characters
the player invests in and cannot keep, because history is closed. This tier is what lets
the cast exceed ten without inflating the endgame party.

### `[NEW]` Thibault — The Clerk

**Class:** Scribe (temporary) · **Era status:** Accountant, third desk · **Fate:** stays in the past
**Working name** — alternatives welcome.

He writes the charter. Not the villain's draft — the *good* one.

Thibault is a moderate, and the moderate position in the revolution is genuinely
defensible: the Terror was real, unlicensed high-output practitioners did real harm, the
Patrimony could return and tithe by force. He writes *"output is a licensed resource"*
believing he is building **a shield** — registration as protection, so nobody is ever
dragged from a house again. Every clause is defensible on its own. That is the horror: the
cage was drafted in good faith by a careful man.

His defection comes when he reads the engrossed copy and finds two sentences he did not
write — the *Aurum* clause — inserted between committee and quorum, in a hand he doesn't
recognize. **He wrote the walls. Someone else wrote the lock.**

- **Core wound:** he is good at his job, and his competence is the mechanism.
- **Coping:** procedure. He believes a correct process can be appealed to. He files
  objections, requests review, follows the rules against the people who wrote them.
- **Won't examine:** whether the shield was ever different from the cage, or whether he
  simply preferred his own handwriting on it.

**Arc:** he tries to get the clause struck. The player knows he fails — the charter exists,
Reid has lived under it for twenty years. Watching a man fight a battle you know he lost is
CT's iron rule converted from mechanic into grief.

**End:** he refuses to sign, so they quiet him — **the first calibration in the city's
history, performed on the author of the document that authorizes it.** He is polite
afterward. He goes back to his desk. He does his job fine. *(That is Margot's description
of her junior practitioner, word for word, two centuries early.)*

**Why he exists — Margot:** the oldest page in her document case is the first consent form.
She finds **his signature on it before she meets him** and assumes he is a monster. Then
she meets a decent man doing careful work. Then she watches the form get used on him. Her
wound is *"I filed the form correctly."* His is *"I wrote the form."* She is the only
person in two hundred years who can tell him what it becomes, and it will not save him.

**Mechanically:** inscribes proto-sigils mid-battle — a one-off inscription kit, affordable
precisely because he is temporary.

### `[NEW]` Sabine — The Almoner

**Class:** White Mage (temporary) · **Era status:** Patrimony tithe-priest, lapsed · **Fate:** stays in the past
**Working name** — alternatives welcome.

Thirty years administering the tithe as **worship**. Not a fraud, not a sadist — she
believed the Deep Patron received what her congregation gave and that the giving meant
something. She held people's hands while they were harvested and told them it was grace.
Some died comforted. She does not think that comfort was nothing.

Her apostasy is not moral. It is the game's Lovecraftian core delivered through a believer:
she did not discover her god was evil. **She discovered it never noticed.** Thirty years of
liturgy addressed to something with no apparatus for knowing her name. She was not lied to
by her god. She was not *heard* by it.

- **Core wound:** she gave her life to something that was never listening, and the people
  she comforted were harvested anyway.
- **Coping:** she keeps the rites. All of them, correctly, pointed at nothing — because the
  rites are the only thing she has that works on people.
- **Won't examine:** whether she stopped believing, or only stopped being able to pretend
  she was heard.

**What she gives the party:** she is the only living person who can read the reliquaries.
The Patrimony's liturgy *is* the harvest's original interface — the rites are an operating
manual. Soraya's reworked kit needs exactly this.

**Why she exists — Karim:** two healers, opposite sins. Karim patched the damage for
decades and won't examine whether that was wisdom or avoidance. Sabine *administered* the
damage for decades and called it care. He wants to be angry and cannot sustain it, because
her hands do the work his hands do. The moment he realizes her medicine is **better than
his** — older, unmetered, uncontaminated — and that she learned it in service of the
harvest, is the strongest argument the game can make about complicity. His verbosity
against her liturgical calm is a register he has never had to play.

**Why she exists — Gideon:** the true believer meets the previous true believer, two
institutions apart. She is what he fears becoming. Neither says so.

**End:** she stays. The revolution ends, the Bureau begins, and she goes on tending people
under new management, knowing it is the same machine with better paperwork, because people
still need hands. Not heroic, not a sacrifice. She keeps working. The most noir ending
available to anyone in the game.

### `[NEW]` `[OPTIONAL]` The Lieutenant — Varenne's second

**Class:** Fighter/Lancer (temporary) · **Fate:** executed, or stays

The General's second, who refuses the gold coat when it is offered to her first. The road
not taken, standing next to the man who took it. Exists to prove the General had a choice —
without her, his fall reads as inevitable, and inevitability is the one thing that would
let him off the hook.

**Cut this if the era cast feels crowded.** Thibault and Sabine carry the thematic load;
the Lieutenant is pure contrast and the General's arc survives without her.

## Tier 3 — Era NPCs (non-playable)

### `[NEW]` General Varenne `[working name]` — the first Gold Coat

The Napoleon figure. The revolution's best soldier, undefeated, beloved, and a
non-practitioner — no Grade, no sigil, he never needed one (a Fighter, in present terms —
deliberately Reid's class). The Accountants court him because the Committee cannot control
what the army loves.

He is taken to the Ruins and shown what the tithe fed. He looks. **It looks back.** Every
later villain in the game descends from that moment.

His speech to the Committee is the origin of every Bureau catechism, delivered once as
conviction rather than propaganda: *the people must be licensed for their own protection;
the tithe returns as a fee; the truth is filed until they are ready.* He is not seduced and
he is not stupid. He understands exactly what he is accepting, and he is **partly right** —
someone does have to administer the crossing. That is what makes it tragedy.

He takes the coat. He thinks it is a promotion. It is a receipt.

**Present-day payoff:** Kurtz wears the same coat, gives the same speech, and does not know
he is an echo. The party meets the original and arrives at the final confrontation already
knowing how the speech ends and what the coat costs.

### `[NEW]` The Registrar — WARDEN's ancestor

Unnamed by design. The Accountant who inserts the *Aurum* clause. No title on any door, no
face the party ever clearly sees, present in every room as handwriting.

**Structural rhyme:** the era has a visible tragic figure (Varenne) and an invisible real
power (the Registrar), exactly as the present has Kurtz and WARDEN. The party learns the
shape of the present conspiracy by seeing its first draft.

### Supporting era figures

- **The Patrimony** `[NEW]` — the pre-Bureau temple-mercantile order. Tithed psychic output
  openly, as religion, to "the Deep Patron." The harvest before it learned to hide.
- **The Accountants** `[NEW]` — old tithe clerks who survived the revolution by changing
  coats. They write everyone's drafts. Nobody notices them because they were always the
  ones who wrote things down. WARDEN's ancestors.
- **The Radicals / The Moderates** `[NEW]` — the two revolutionary factions. Both quote the
  broadsheet. The lie gets built out of true words.
